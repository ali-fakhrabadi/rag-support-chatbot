# Product Care Guide

Taking care of outdoor gear makes it last longer and keeps the warranty valid, because damage
caused by improper care is not covered.

## Down sleeping bags

Wash a down sleeping bag in a front-loading machine on a gentle cold cycle with a down-specific
soap. Never use a top-loading machine with an agitator. Tumble dry on low heat with three clean
tennis balls to break up clumps; drying usually takes 3 to 4 hours. Store the bag uncompressed
in its large cotton storage sack, not in the stuff sack.

## Tents

Never machine wash a tent. Clean it by hand with a sponge and lukewarm water. Always dry the tent
completely before storing it to prevent mildew. Re-apply seam sealer every 2 years or when seams
start to leak.

## Waterproof jackets

Wash waterproof jackets with a technical cleaner, not regular detergent or fabric softener. To
restore the water-repellent finish, tumble dry the jacket on medium heat for 20 minutes after
washing.

## Hiking boots

Clean boots after every trip and dry them at room temperature. Keep wet boots at least 2 feet
(60 cm) away from fires, stoves and heaters, because direct heat cracks leather and weakens glue.
Condition leather boots every 6 months.

## Trekking poles

After hiking in wet conditions, take the pole sections apart and let them dry separately so the
locking mechanisms do not corrode.
